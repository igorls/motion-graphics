# motion-graphics skill: working on it

This repo IS the skill (`skills/motion-graphics/`). The goal: a strong starting point for any agent to make excellent motion graphics with any audio and video models. We improve it by dogfooding: making real pieces for real (ideally open-source) projects and folding what we learn back in.

## Principles

- **Model-agnostic core, specific adapters.** Principles go in `references/*.md`; anything about one model (node names, prompt formats, measured behaviour) goes in `references/adapters/<model>-<host>.md`. Never let one model's quirks leak into the core workflow.
- **Measured, not assumed.** Adapter notes record what was actually observed (settings, timings, failure modes), with the date and hardware class.
- **No legal opinions in the skill.** The skill records provenance (`assets.json`: model, checkpoint, prompt, seed) and leaves licence decisions to the user.
- **Deterministic renders.** Generation happens once into `public/`; the engine renders every frame as a pure function of time.
- **Brand-neutral.** Brand specifics live in profiles; private/client ones in `profiles/local/` (gitignored).

## Dev loop

1. Change the skill: `SKILL.md`, `references/`, or the engine in `template/`.
2. Test the template in a scratch piece: `cp -r skills/motion-graphics/template dogfood/<name> && cd dogfood/<name> && bun install`, then `bunx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.json` and `bun scripts/render.ts sheet --n 16 --cols 8`. Look at the PNGs.
3. Dogfood for real: in another project, ask for a piece; the installed skill (junction to this repo) is used. Note what the agent got wrong or had to improvise, and fix the skill, not just the piece.
4. Keep `SKILL.md` frontmatter valid YAML (no `: ` inside the plain `description` value; that silently breaks skill discovery).
5. Commit with conventional messages (`feat:`, `fix:`, `docs:`); no generated media in the repo.

## Dogfood log

- **2026-09-28, wormdb launch Reel (5/10 by the user).** Coherent "printout comes alive" concept, true claims, clean type, on the beat; but four flat text slides with a locked camera, empty lower frames, no depth or money shot, 23 minutes end to end, and it copied the adapter's example melody. Fixes: studio-style checkpoints with the user (brief interview, concept choice, style-frame approval), three concepts with money shots, shot list rules (vary size/layout, depth, transformation), style frames before animation, fresh-eyes art-director review with a scored rubric (review.md), the bar and shot vocabulary in craft.md, a 3D stage + fly-through example in the engine, a motif-composition method for music, and a generic ComfyUI batch runner (scripts/comfy.ts).
- **2026-09-28, round 2: meshrooms launch film (7/10 by the user) and wormdb open-source Reel (6-7/10 by the user: "clean, the music matched, but it lacked wow factor").** The new process worked in both: 8-12 brief questions, three concepts with money shots chosen with the user, original two-draft scores, style-frame approval, fresh-eyes review rounds (3 and 10). Runs took ~55 min and ~1 h 40 min. Remaining gap: grey-box materials (Craft stuck at 4-6 in every round; in wormdb it never moved in 10 rounds because every fix went to camera/composition), headlines on busy backgrounds propped up by scrims, frame 0 as a plain page (wormdb hook), the concept's core device diluted (meshrooms), reviewers without history contradicting each other, and both shipped below 8 without asking. Fixes: look development (engine/looks.ts: customLook + halftone/gooch/rimGlow/holo, glass/metal/iridescent, addRim/addGrain; stage lighting + paper; lookdev turntable; references/lookdev.md), a Look & materials review dimension, reviewers get the review log, plateau rule (structural change when a dimension doesn't move in two rounds), ship gate that asks the user after ~5 rounds, calm composed areas for type, frame 0 designed like the money shot, the core device named in the treatment and checked in review.
- **Wow factor (follow-up to round 2).** Both pieces had a money *frame* but no moment worth replaying: their drops were spent on camera moves over static geometry. Fixes: a wow moment (a motion event on the music's peak) required in every concept, prototyped first and polished most; a "Wow" review dimension capped at 6 when there's no replayable moment; craft.md "Wow: engineering the peak" patterns; engine/swarm.ts (thousands of instances morphing between formations, text/logo sampling) and scenes/assemble.ts.
- **Round 3: NInfer debut video (X, 16:9): 8/10 by the user ("the wow factor is there").** First run with wow moments and lookdev in the skill: 8 brief questions, three concepts each with a core device, money shot and wow moment (built on recorded real model probabilities), the peak (18 ghost cube futures collapsing on the drop) built first, a custom phosphor shader of its own, honest labelling questions (3 of 24 solves; cooperative framing vs a competitor). It asked before using the live engine, the shared GPU and downloading fonts. Gap it hit: the beat tracker put the first downbeat at 0.47 s, so it measured section onsets by hand; fixed with analyze_audio.py --bpm/--first-beat/--sections. Skipped the lookdev turntable (went straight to a custom shader in the scenes). Result after 4 review rounds (~1 h 35 min): Concept 8, Wow 8, Variety 8, Brand 8, Motion 7 (from 4, after rebuilding every cut as a continuous hand-off), Composition 7, Look 6, Craft 6; stopped and put the remaining concept-level gap to the user; refused a reviewer suggestion that would have invented numbers. Fixes after it: real UI restaged in the world's materials (lookdev.md), frame 0 built with the money shot's light and density, strongest-model reviewer, and the gate (not the reviewer's yes) decides shipping.
- **Music first (user direction after round 3).** Models can't hear audio, so the workflow now secures the track right after the concept and turns it into a music map before the treatment: analyze_audio.py writes MUSIC-MAP.md (sections, per-bar energy/density/brightness, drops/stops/builds/breakdowns/peak/tail, wow candidate, cut points, reading holds, strongest hits) and out/music-map.png, and audio.json gets ranked moments (au.moment('drop'), au.section()). Verified on three tracks: NInfer's (drop after a stop at 12.04 s found automatically, matching the agent's hand measurement), the 116 BPM YuE test (drop 8.37 s), and wormdb's picked take, where the map shows the peak at bar 6 while that agent planned bar 5: the mismatch the map exists to catch. Phase fitting moved to a circular mean of the strongest kicks (the onset-envelope fit locked onto offbeats).
- **Round 4: NInfer launch refresh ("your chat model is already a decision engine", 16:9 + 1:1, about 40 s): "beautiful" by the user, after their notes rather than the reviewer's.** The process held: brief and concept rounds, recorded runs from the live engine (asked first), a YuE2 score, a custom shader world. The first 20 s cut went through five fresh-review rounds and stalled below the gate (best Concept 7 · Wow 7 · Craft 6). The reviewers flagged collisions, clipped cards and unreadable fine print. Four structural problems surfaced only in the user's notes:
  - "all too fast" → longer, with labelled 0.25x slow motion;
  - "hard to focus on what is going on in the beginning, with all happening at once" → each client introduced alone from its own recorded phase, then all four together, with the music adding an instrument per chapter;
  - "not real tetris … the pieces are not falling down" → real Tetris grammar (spawn, gravity, lock, line clear, NEXT), timed to the recorded answers;
  - "the readability on the text overlays can improve", "sometimes it's mixing too much in the background" → the subject framed between the overlay bands at the tightest camera point, the world dimmed behind it, opaque panels, haloed captions, plated readouts.

  The user also found renders slow. Measured on a 2 s slice (60 frames at 4 sub-frames): about 60 ms of scene per sub-frame, the encoder preset irrelevant. Drawing frame-quantized canvases once per frame cut that slice 2.7x, and the busiest one 3.3x.

  Fixes: a first-watch comprehension law and review dimension, motion strips and reading-time tables in the review evidence, introduce-then-combine, recorded data as motion, borrowed systems obey their own rules, overlay and background rules (the halo rule revised for live 3D/data worlds), a placeholder-data watermark protocol, preview encoding, `Layer2D.clearFor`, frame-snapped stills, `sheet --every/--crop/--samples`, `check` across formats.

## Status and next ideas

- Score trajectory by the user: 5 (round 1) → 7 / 6-7 (round 2) → 8 (round 3) → "beautiful" (round 4, after the user's notes). The wow-moment rule was the biggest single lift; round 4's lesson is that comprehension and pacing are invisible to a stills-based review.
- Next dogfood should check: does the comprehension dimension (with motion strips and the reading-time table) catch pacing and "too much at once" before the user does? Is "introduce, then combine" applied unprompted for multi-part messages? Do agents reach for `clearFor` on frame-quantized layers and `check` across formats before sending cuts? Does the agent map the music before the treatment and plan windows on the map (au.moment)? Do the round-3 fixes (real UI restaged in the world, frame 0 with the money shot's light, gate over reviewer) lift Look/Craft past 6? Does a Codex run follow the same process? Does every concept name a wow moment, and is it prototyped first? Does the agent build a lookdev turntable and write a custom shader before the style frames? Does Look/Craft reach 8? Does the review log keep reviewers consistent? Does it ask before shipping below 8?
- Verified end to end: 3D fly-through stage, ComfyUI batch runner, kinetic type, before/after wipe, keyed clip insert with depth, end card, adaptive motion blur, poster bake, 9:16 and 4:5, score-conditioned music (YuE2) and green-screen clips (MiniMax FastH3).
